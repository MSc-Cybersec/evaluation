## Bottom line

A 90-day exception is not automatically permissible; it must be treated as a formal, time-bounded security deviation and approved **before the affected change or service release**. Per **GOV-G-006**: record the control being waived, technical justification, residual risk and accepting owner, compensating controls, exact expiry date, and independent approval. The decision required is to approve with conditions, reject, or defer release until the mandatory control is satisfied.

## Risk and impact

**High risk.** An undocumented or open-ended exception creates unowned and potentially permanent non-compliance, leaving the legacy service, its data, users, and connected systems exposed to the threat addressed by the omitted control.

The actual residual risk cannot be established until the team identifies:

- The specific mandatory control and failure condition.
- Affected service, environments, interfaces, and data.
- Threat and business-impact scenarios.
- Duration and exposure during the requested 90 days.
- Effectiveness and limitations of compensating controls.
- A funded remediation or retirement plan.

The risk rating of the underlying control must also be considered; it has not been provided.

## Required actions

1. **Create the formal deviation before release — Delivery team**
   - Identify the exact mandatory control and affected service.
   - Describe the technical limitation preventing compliance.
   - Define the requested start date and exact expiry date, limited to the approved 90-day period.
   - Link the deviation to the service and planned release.

2. **Assess and assign residual risk — Risk owner**
   - Document credible threat scenarios, affected assets and data, likelihood, business impact, and residual risk after compensating controls.
   - Name the person accepting the residual risk; a team or distribution list is insufficient for accountable ownership.

3. **Implement compensating controls — Service owner and security engineering**
   - Document each compensating control, its coverage, limitations, operational owner, and verification method.
   - Confirm that the controls are active before the exception becomes effective.
   - Where compensation is inadequate, reduce exposure or do not approve the exception.

4. **Provide a dated remediation plan — Service owner**
   - Define the work required to restore compliance or retire/isolate the legacy service.
   - Record accountable owners, milestones, dependencies, funding, and completion date within the exception period.

5. **Obtain independent approval — Independent security/risk authority**
   - The approver must be outside the delivery team.
   - Approval must explicitly cover the residual risk, compensating controls, affected scope, and expiry.
   - Approval must occur before any release that relies on the exception.

6. **Enforce the exception through the release gate — DevSecOps or release management**
   - Require an approved, linked, and unexpired deviation reference whenever the mandatory check is overridden.
   - Do not allow an expired, ownerless, or unapproved exception to bypass the control.

7. **Review on or before expiry — Risk owner and independent approver**
   - Close the exception after remediation, stop the affected activity, or submit a newly justified decision.
   - Renewal is not automatic and must not become indefinite.
